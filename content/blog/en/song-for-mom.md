## What goes into a song for Mom

The best songs live on small, real details. These questions help you collect them:

- **What do you call her?** Mom, Mommy, Mum or a nickname all your own?
- **What do you remember from your childhood?** Her signature dish, bedtime stories, day trips, vacations.
- **What is typical of her?** Her sayings, her quirks, her hobbies. The garden, baking, the Sunday phone calls.
- **What are you grateful for?** A moment when she was there for you when it mattered.
- **Who belongs in it?** Siblings, Dad, grandchildren: everyone who should appear in the chorus.

Bullet points are completely fine. You don't have to rhyme anything. From them come verses and a chorus that sticks.

## Example: What your information could look like

> *"Mom's name is Petra, she's turning 60. We call her 'Mutti'. She raised the three of us kids almost on her own, bakes apple cake every Sunday, loves her garden and Udo Jürgens. Her saying: 'It'll be all right.' She now has two grandchildren, Mia and Ben. We want to say thank you."*

That's all it takes. From this comes a song with her name, her saying in the chorus and the grandchildren in the last verse.

## Which music style suits Mom?

- **Emotional pop ballad:** piano and strings, when it should move her.
- **Acoustic / Singer-Songwriter:** warm and close, like a letter in song form.
- **Modern Schlager:** German-style sing-along pop, for moms who love to sing along and dance.
- **Pop & Radio Hit:** cheerful and modern, good for younger moms.

For the voice, many people choose a female voice or a duet. A duet can sound like a conversation between child and mother, for example.
